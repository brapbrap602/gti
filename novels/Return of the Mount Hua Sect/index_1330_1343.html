Bang!

Each step unleashed an explosive boom.

This was not the Yu Yiseol who was always quiet and nimble like a swallow. Urgency she could not hide emanated from her now.

‘Faster!’

Her expressionless face contorted more and more as her anxiety grew.

Whoooosh!

Accelerating so the scenery blurred around her, she ran, slicing off the neck of an Evil Sect member who got in her way in an instant.

There was something about the sword strike she gave now that resembled Chung Myung’s.

‘Faster!’

She kept stamping the ground.

Attached to her sword was not just her own life. It wasn’t only the lives of the Southern Island sect disciples struggling under the rain of arrows flying from behind her.

Chung Myung’s lonely back, standing alone to hold the line after sending her on, kept haunting her. If she failed to clean this up in time and return, that back might be the last image of Chung Myung she would ever see.

‘Too slow!’

Yu Yiseol, seized by impatience, stamped the ground again hard.

Kwang!

Softness and calm were strengths unique to Yu Yiseol that no one in Mount Hua possessed. It was the path she had chosen to avoid falling behind those born with strength.

A sword style not beholden to physical power, one that could be built through bone-grinding effort even if not innate.

That was the blade of the sword ghost Yu Yiseol.

Shriiiing!

A force-infused heavy arrow flew at tremendous speed. She lowered her body fully without slowing. It was almost as if she were skimming the ground.

The fiercely shot arrow sliced through the air in vain.

Wham! Wham!

Arrows came in succession, but Yu Yiseol’s technique would not permit a single arrow to touch her body.

Yu Yiseol cut the throat of the archer nocking the bowstring in one stroke.

Swoosh!

Her blade traced a graceful curve and, in an instant, cut through the archer’s upraised bow and severed the carotid. It was a single, marvelous stroke that drew gasps from anyone.

Yet Yu Yiseol bit her lip so hard it bled.

‘Too slow!’

This was wrong. She had to be faster. More efficient.

I think I understand.

Why Chung Myung’s sword was so often frantic. Why there was no leisure in his blade.

‘Faster!’

She bit the flesh inside her cheek and ran toward another spot where arrows had been fired.

She knew, of course. The people she sought were not there. Those firing the heavy arrows were still hidden to avoid revealing their positions.

But she had no time to hunt them down one by one now.

Then there was only one method.

Instead of her finding them, make them find her.

If she fought most loudly at the center and slaughtered many enemies, those who couldn’t stand it would charge at her.

Then, at the cost of exposing herself to danger, she could find the enemies and lure attacks away from the main force...

Grit!

The thought flashing through her head made Yu Yiseol involuntarily bite her lip hard.

It was something she had once blurted out thoughtlessly. She hadn’t understood Chung Myung then, and had no intention of trying to understand him; that careless remark finally lodged in her chest now.

It wasn’t that she distrusted the sahyung sajaes. She had never doubted their skill.

Still, she must overextend herself.

Because that way she could save even one more person.

Still, she had to push herself.

So the enemies would target her and not others.

Fighting most brilliantly at the front was simply the best choice. The more she leapt into the enemy center and drew attention with her blade, the safer everyone else became.

She saw enemies arrayed up ahead.

Seeing her rush headlong without looking back or ahead, the archers’ faces went dark.

“Shoot! Fire!”

The archers twisted their upraised bows and aimed at Yu Yiseol. The arrows nocked several per bow rained down on her like a storm.

Instead of slowing, Yu Yiseol drove her sword into the ground. The resilient Plum Blossom Sword bent as if to break and then snapped back. Using that force, she leapt into the air.

Screeeech!

A speeding arrow grazed her pantleg, but unfazed, Yu Yiseol shot up like a raptor and thrust herself into the midst of the enemies.

The Plum Blossom Sword traced a far rougher, fiercer arc than her usual trajectory.

Kwagagagak!

A steel bow was cut off along with a body. As the split bow and torso flew apart, blood splattered above her head.

Bang!

As soon as her foot hit the ground she struck, quickly plunging her sword into the throat of a startled, recoiling archer. There was a gruesome cracking sound as the throat was pierced; the blade protruding from the nape was red.

“Kill, kill her!”

“She’s alone anyway! Kill her!”

There’s a common belief that once an archer’s distance is closed, he can do nothing.

But a well-known saying isn’t always true. An archer with his string released can still wield a powerful short-staff-like weapon, and close distance doesn’t mean he can’t shoot.

In fact, as the distance shortens, arrows can become even more devastating to the target.

The archers surged forward, swinging their short, strung crossbows. Those behind them held their bowstrings taut, ready to fire at any opening.

Kraaang!

Yu Yiseol deflected a steel crossbow aimed at her head in an instant. But before she could brace her blade again, another archer leapt up nearby and fired a force-infused arrow at point-blank range.

Without thinking, Yu Yiseol leaned back. The arrow whistled past right above her shoulder, tearing a few strands of hair, and mercilessly pierced the belly of a Four Sects Alliance man who was charging from behind.

“Kyaaaargh!”

The fired arrow had harmed an ally, but no one cared. Archers who surged up alongside continued to rain swift shots at Yu Yiseol.

Faced with countless arrows pouring in at close range, all Yu Yiseol could do was roll her body and dodge.

Papapapat!

The spot she rolled out of was instantly packed with arrows. If she’d been a moment slower, the outcome would’ve been obvious.

‘Different.’

Yu Yiseol sprang up like lightning and charged the nearest visible enemy. Her body moved more readily than she expected.

“U-uh!”

Shing!

After driving her sword into the startled enemy’s belly, Yu Yiseol grabbed his throat, yanked hard, and spun him. In an instant he rolled and their positions swapped.

“Ah, no...”

Thrown into that forced reversal, he sensed his approaching fate and frantically turned his head.

Kwaduk! Kwadudduk!

Before his head had fully turned, arrows rained and embedded themselves all over his back. Using his body as a shield against the arrows, Yu Yiseol drew her sword and cut down others immediately.

The moment you leap into a pack of wolves, you must be prepared to pay the price. No matter how fierce, one cannot come out of their midst entirely unscathed.

Yu Yiseol knew that well. Doing the impossible out of desperation would be nothing but suicide.

And yet there was only one reason she leapt into the wolf pack’s center.

“Aaaaaaah!”

Several of the group surrounding Yu Yiseol let out screams. They were helplessly struck by a blade from behind. They died without even seeing who had cut them.

“Sago!”

It was Yoon Jong. He had calmly but swiftly broken the enemy lines from the outside.

As harrowing screams kept sounding from outside the encirclement, the archers’ hands trembled slightly.

A bow, unlike a spear or sword, can only aim at one place. There is no bow that can fire at enemies surrounding you front and back at once.

As if to prove that an archer is weakest not when the distance shortens but when the directions to aim become mixed, two Mount Hua people scattered sword qi from inside and outside simultaneously.

The splendidly blooming Plum Blossom Sword qi swept through those who had gathered to encircle Yu Yiseol.

“Aaaaah!”

“Kraaaaah!”

Enemies riddled with holes fell limply like old rag dolls.

Momentum had now shifted to Yu Yiseol. She seized it and began slaughtering enemies in an instant.

Whoooosh!

But then, a whistling sound rose high. A heavy arrow of terrifying speed flew toward Yu Yiseol and Yoon Jong. It was clearly of a different caliber than the arrows the archers here had been shooting.

At last, having found the traces of those she had to chase, Yu Yiseol’s eyes flashed a deadly blue.

But before she could push off the ground, someone was already running in that direction.

“Gul?”

Jo Gul, his face unusually cold, dashed into the forest like a heroic guard. It didn’t take long to learn the result. A death shriek that was clearly not Jo Gul’s was heard.

Once. Again.

A total of three screams burst out. The moment the heavy arrows were fired, Jo Gul had precisely located and memorized their positions, hunted them down, and turned them all into ‘prey’.

It was a calmness and decisiveness hardly recognizable in the Jo Gul everyone knew.

Despair filled the archers’ eyes when they realized even the supporting warriors had been taken out.

Naturally, as the assault slackened, Yu Yiseol staggered and clutched her stomach.

“Sa-Sago!”

Startled, Yoon Jong hurriedly grabbed her.

Blood seeped through the gaps of the hand clutching her belly. When she had used the enemy’s body as a shield to stop an arrow, that arrow which pierced the body had left a wound in Yu Yiseol’s abdomen.

“You bastards!”

A furious curse burst from Yoon Jong’s mouth. He swung his sword violently and sliced the archers apart.

“Aaaaaah!”

The shattered momentum could not be recovered. Judging survival more important than orders, the archers abandoned their comrades and fled without looking back.

“Sago! Are you all right?”

Yoon Jong did not pursue them and instead supported Yu Yiseol. Checking on her mattered far more than cutting down those scoundrels.

“Yoon Jong.”

“Sago, y-you’re wounded! You need treatment at once...”

“There are still cannons.”

“What?”

“Trust me!”

Yu Yiseol shoved Yoon Jong away and stamped the ground at the same time.

“Sago! No, you can’t! Sago! Sago!”

Yoon Jong called out desperately, but Yu Yiseol ran full tilt toward the rear, leaving the front to Yoon Jong and Jo Gul, hoping Chung Myung would not fall before she arrived.

Her throat burned with anxiety.

Wherever she stepped, the ground was stained red with the blood she left behind.

“Oh, they’re coming!”

“D-damn it! Fire! Shoot and kill them!”

The gunner who had been aiming the cannon shouted with a pale face.

Of course, even as he shouted, he knew his words made no sense. A cannon is not an anti-personnel weapon. It is devastating against clustered targets, but a cannon cannot hit a single person precisely.

Moreover, the one storming in was no ordinary person; if he was a Mount Hua warrior who had sliced down their protectors like demons, a mere cannon surely wouldn’t work.

“I said fire, now!”

Kraaaang!

The cannon belched fire at Jo Gul, who was charging with gritted teeth. A black shell flew out at terrifying speed.

But Jo Gul didn’t even blink and kept running. The shell skimmed dangerously over his head and struck the large old tree behind him.

Kuuuuuuuuuung!

A tree thicker than a human torso snapped with a crack. It was a sight that showed the cannon’s true power.

But no matter how powerful an attack, it is meaningless if it doesn’t hit.

“A-again! Quickly!”

Hands hurriedly reloaded the cannon like lightning.

But uselessly so; the moment the fuse was relit, Jo Gul had already reached them.

“Th-this...!”

Kwang!

Stepping into his stance, Jo Gul lashed his sword with force.

“You bastards!”

Paaaang!

With a sound like tearing air, a red line was sharply drawn through the void. Those attached to the cannon were split in two and collapsed instantly.

“S-someone save me...”

Puk!

Jo Gul drove his sword into the chest of someone the blade had barely reached, and before he could even feel the heartbeat at the tip, he violently withdrew it.

Sshhh!

Crimson blood sprayed and splattered onto the cannon.

“Huhk! Huhk! Huhk!”

Jo Gul panted harshly and looked around. There was no one alive in sight. Only blood-soaked corpses cooling remained.

Grit.

Biting his lip, he stamped on the still-smoldering cannon. The muzzle jerked up toward the sky.

Kraaaang!

The shell shot up high into the sky.

Kraaaang!

Seeing the shell soaring upward, Baek Cheon instantly realized the situation and shouted loudly.

“Everyone move! Now!”

The situation was dire. More than half the elders had fallen to poisoning and injury, and Southern Island’s disciples were numerous wounded.

Only those too injured to run properly and those whose stamina was utterly spent were barely holding onto their swords.

But even in that terrible state, everyone responded to Baek Cheon’s words. The voice they’d heard constantly from Southern Island’s shore until now had now held absolute power over Southern Island’s disciples.

“This way!”

Baek Cheon shouted and took the lead in running.

For the exhausted, it’s easier to recognize an action and follow than to understand words.

“Follow the Acting Sect Leader!”

“Move, didn’t you hear! Do you want to die here?”

When Namgung Do-Wei and Hae Yeon barked sharply, Geum Yang-Baek shouted loudly.

“What are you doing! Immediately follow the Acting Sect Leader! Elders, carry the wounded disciples on your backs! Now!”

Even though it was remarkable they were still standing, Southern Island’s elders did not forget their duty. The elders who somehow hoisted the immobile wounded followed behind Baek Cheon, led by Geum Yang-Baek.

The Southern Island disciples who could still move reflexively followed and drove their feet into the ground at full speed.

“Damn it! What are you doing!”

Meanwhile, Im So-Byeong raged and grabbed Tang Soso from behind. She was clinging to a dying Southern Island disciple.

“Let me go! I just need to finish this bandage!”

“He’s already dead! He’s already!!”

“Not yet... he’s not dead yet!”

“Snap out of it! If you die, the rest will all die too!”

Tang Soso’s eyes wavered at that. Just seeing them showed how emotionally pushed to the limit she was.

But Im So-Byeong paid no heed to her feelings and forcibly dragged her along.

As everyone ran out, Gwak Hwan-So ran over to those who still couldn’t move.

“What are you doing! Follow immediately!”

“Great sahyung!”

Those who didn’t know what to do looked at Gwak Hwan-So with despair filling their eyes.

“T-the sahyungs’ wounds...”

Gwak Hwan-So’s eyes dropped. A priest lay moaning with a blood-stained bandage wrapped around his heart.

Understanding the situation, Gwak Hwan-So involuntarily clenched his fist.

The other wounded could be moved somehow. They could die together if need be.

But not these ones. They couldn’t be carried. Their wounds were too grave; moving them recklessly would make the wounds burst open, hastening their deaths instead.

Tang Soso had done emergency treatment to keep them alive, but those with no strength left for the forced march remained.

Gwak Hwan-So gritted his teeth. The tendons in his jaw stood out.

“Go!”

“Yes?”

“I said go, now!”

“G-go where? Sahyung!”

Someone who grasped what Gwak Hwan-So meant shouted desperately.

“They’re still alive, still! What kind of nonsense is that? Are you saying we should leave the sahyungs here like this? That’s...!”

“Shut up and go, didn’t I say!”

“Great sahyung!”

When the sajae screamed, Gwak Hwan-So grabbed him by the collar and yanked him roughly. His eyes, bloodshot, glared fiercely at the priest whose eyes were wet with tears.

“Then what? Will you die here together?”

No answer came. He shoved the sajae away as if throwing him off. There was no time for scuffling.

If Chung Myung, who was holding the rear, were pushed back and the gorge reopened, the Ten Thousand People Clan’s hordes would pour out from there like a torrent.

These exhausted people wouldn’t even be able to resist. They’d be cut down without properly swinging their swords.

They had barely escaped a hellish place; they wouldn’t accept such a miserable death!

“Go, now! If you don’t want to die by my hand!”

Whether cowed by Gwak Hwan-So’s ferocity or realizing it was inevitable, the prostrate disciple let out a wailing cry and ran forward.

Those who hesitated until the end also eventually followed him.

Gwak Hwan-So, who had been holding back his tears with his head tilted up, lowered his gaze again.

Even in that brief moment, Gwak Hwan-So was terrified.

They were injured but conscious, so they must have heard everything he said. Their eyes full of resentment, their words like sharp daggers; it was terrifying.

But what Gwak Hwan-So saw was far different from what he’d expected.

“S-sahyung...”

Some pale-faced disciples who had collapsed pointed to themselves with trembling hands.

Gwak Hwan-So was speechless. The moment he understood what it meant, the heavy weight on his shoulders finally collapsed horribly.

“P-please...”

“Ugh...”

He wanted to block his ears. He wanted to bite a blade and fall over. He wanted to run away anywhere.

“S-sahyung...”

“Aaaaaaaaah!”

Gwak Hwan-So let out a howl and swung his sword like lightning. Blood welled from his split lips.

His beloved blade, clearly engraved with the wave pattern symbolizing Southern Island Sect, sliced through the sahyung brothers’ throats in an instant.

Poooot!

Blood spurted from the cut throats, and on the faces of the Southern Island disciples whose eyes were dimming, faint smiles bloomed.

“Th-thank...”

“S-survive...”

Gwak Hwan-So didn’t stay to hear the rest and bolted. No, it’s more accurate to say he couldn’t bear to listen and ran.

Farther, farther away.

Tears no longer came.

He knew there was no choice. They could no longer fight, yet they couldn’t simply die easily.

But if he left them like this, they’d be captured by the enraged Ten Thousand People Clan and suffer every humiliation and die painfully.

In that case, it was better to cut their lives short with his own hands.

So this was a way of helping them. Gwak Hwan-So knew that.

“Aaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaa!”

But knowing doesn’t erase the heart-wrenching pain. Knowing doesn’t change the fact he had cut the throats of brothers he’d spent his life with.

‘Survive.’

Thoughts of an honorable death no longer remained even slightly in his mind.

He must survive.

He must survive and avenge this. For the number who died here... no, he must shed a hundred, a thousand times their blood to repay their enemies.

‘I will survive by any means! I must!’

As he drove his feet into the ground with all his strength, a murderous poison flared in his eyes.

“Sasuk!”

“Ahead!”

“We haven’t located all the archers! There could be more ambushes ahead!”

Yoon Jong answered as if shouting and quickly joined Baek Cheon at his side.

A moment later, Jo Gul also ran up at full speed from ahead.

“All the cannons are dealt with!”

“Take the lead! There may be more ambushes!”

“Sago! Sago is heading to the rear! She’s been injured!”

Baek Cheon involuntarily turned to the direction Jo Gul had mentioned.

They had already entered a forest path, and in the distance through the undergrowth a deep gorge lay like it had been split by a god’s sword.

You didn’t need more explanation to guess what had happened.

“Chung Myung is probably at his limit too! And the injured Sago can’t manage alone! We have to help!”

For a moment, conflict clouded Baek Cheon’s face.

He must go.

Just hearing Jo Gul made the situation clear. And right now, the only one with the capacity to go back was Baek Cheon.

But if he pulled himself away and the ones left behind ran into the ambush ahead, could they break through on their own?

“...Jo Gul.”

“Yes, sasuk!”

“Take the front. Lead them away like this.”

“Then will the sasuk go?”

“...”

“Sasuk?”

Baek Cheon was silent. For a moment Jo Gul’s eyes trembled.

“Sasuk! S-Sago’s injury is serious! This isn’t the usual behaviour of Sago!”

“I told you to take the front, lead them!”

Baek Cheon’s sudden angry shout made Jo Gul flinch.

But only briefly; Jo Gul glared at Baek Cheon with bloodshot eyes, his face as if he might lunge and throw a punch any second.

“So you’re leaving them behind?”

“They’re not men who can’t take care of their own bodies! They’ll definitely come back!”

“You said you’d run away, even if it meant abandoning everyone else in danger!”

Baek Cheon fell silent as if at a loss for words. The neat corner of his eye trembled.

“You told me! You clearly told me that, sasuk!”

“...”

“Chung Myung will die! Sago will die! Don’t you understand the situation? Has your head gone mad!”

“Shut your mouth!”

The shout came not from Baek Cheon but from Yoon Jong. Yoon Jong glared at Jo Gul with a face twisted like a demon.

“Sahyung. But...”

“Didn’t I tell you to shut up!”

When Yoon Jong shouted again, Jo Gul’s hand gripping his sword trembled faintly.

Jo Gul’s gaze desperately flicked to the Southern Island faction following them. But his eyes were no longer the same as before.

“Who the hell do these people think they are...”

“Jo Gul!”

“Damn Southern Island, why do we have to go this far for them! You’re all crazy! You’re out of your minds, you bastards!”

“You damned bastard!”

As Yoon Jong grabbed for his collar, Jo Gul didn’t hold back and struck his hand away. Leaving a startled Yoon Jong behind, he ran toward the gorge.

“Stop him! Jo Gul, you bastard!”

“Let him go.”

Yoon Jong turned at Baek Cheon’s words. Baek Cheon bit his lips until they bled and cast a terrifying frenzy with his eyes.

“...You take the lead and open the way. Arrive in the Norther Lands alive. No matter what happens.”

“...Yes, sasuk.”

It sounded as if something somewhere was breaking.

Maybe it was a sign of catastrophe.

Baek Cheon’s hand, hidden inside his sleeve where others couldn’t see, trembled pitiably.

Seeing the scene spread out before him, Gwi Yang’s face went deathly pale.

He was certainly a man accustomed to blood. Since the day he first took a sword in hand, the days he had seen blood outnumbered those he hadn’t.

Even he felt strange at the sight now before him.

The white cliffs rising on both sides of the gorge were stained red as if purposely painted, and the owners of that blood were none other than the members he had painstakingly raised.

Looking at the gruesome corpses left behind, Gwi Yang involuntarily swallowed hard.

Of the hundred or so Blood Sword Squad members, more than half had vanished here as wandering spirits. In fact, among those who survived, nearly half were barely clinging to life—merely waiting for the death that approached.

There is a simple word for this situation.

Destruction.

A unit of the Ten Thousand People Clan, the Blood Sword Troops, had collapsed here. Only about thirty remained. It was almost laughable to still call it a unit.

He didn’t have warm feelings of cherishing those he had raised, but looking at his subordinates’ mangled bodies stirred an indescribable feeling.

He stared absentmindedly at the figure blocking the gorge’s exit, preventing them from escaping to the wide plains.

This was the one who, alone, held back not only the Blood Sword Squad but the Ten Thousand People Clan’s thousand-strong force.

It was Mount Hua’s Sword Saint.

Perhaps, this scene would one day become legend, even a myth retold in the Jianghu for a long time.

When later generations discussed Mount Hua’s Sword Saint, this might be the first anecdote they brought up.

Even if Mount Hua’s Sword Saint buried his bones here and turned to a wisp of spirit, his heroic stature would not fade.

No, he might even gain a fame greater than if he returned alive.

Sometimes a hero is completed by his death.

Gwi Yang could not deny it. Though an enemy, Chung Myung’s deeds and path were undeniably great.

He had rushed all the way here to rescue the Southern Island Sect trapped behind enemy lines, and even stood alone against the Ten Thousand People Clan’s force which was pursuing Southern Island Sect.

That plain, unembellished fact alone forced acknowledgment of heroic conduct. Perhaps Gwi Yang was witnessing the birth of a legend to be passed down in the Jianghu.

But in the actual appearance of the one before him now, it was hard to find heroic majesty or the bearing of a mythic protagonist.

The once-highly tied hair was disheveled; the youthful face stained dark crimson with his own blood and that of the Four Sects Alliance.

His left thigh was split open to the bone, and a clear mark where a sword had pierced just left of his sternum remained.

A temporarily bound flank had split open again, gushing dark blood, and a long cut ran from his left neck to his armpit.

The wound on his right leg from when he entered here, the sword scar crossing his back almost invisible to the eye, and countless other small injuries were too many to count.

How could anyone find heroic stature in the figure barely supporting himself on the sword he had thrust into the ground, looking as if his breath could leave him at any moment?

Did that unbearably pitiful sight possess the grandeur to adorn a myth?

That’s not a hero.

That’s just a human.

A human flailing, raging, and screaming.

There was no hero here who split mountains with one stroke and made the heavens tremble with his roar. Only a man paying a brutal price for what he had done existed.

Drip.

Blood trickled and fell drop by drop from Chung Myung’s chin. From his slightly parted lips, a faint breath escaped as if it might break at any moment.

To anyone watching, he had already passed his limit.

He looked like a wounded beast, incapable of resistance. Even a common Ten Thousand People Clan warrior, not a Blood Sword Squad member, could likely end his life now without much trouble.

But the Blood Sword Troops members, who took more joy than anything in ending their prey, dared not approach the wounded beast now.

Yet Gwi Yang did not want to blame them. In fact, he couldn’t blame them.

It was at that moment.

Screech.

When Chung Myung clenched the sword hard, a sound of friction leaked out. Gripping the sword planted in the ground, he slowly straightened his bent body, moving like an old cart creaking.

Gwi Yang could not speak; he simply watched the scene.

Blood continued to flow from Chung Myung as he finally straightened. His lips, visible between matted hair, slowly opened.

He was saying something, but it was hard to hear.

It seemed the voice would not come easily even with effort. Chung Myung finally pressed his lips shut. When they met and separated, the parched lips split and bled.

“...on...”

The violet lips, split, were soaked red with blood. Moments later, a hoarse, cracked voice leaked out as if it might break at any second.

“Keep... going?”

The corner of his mouth barely tilted into a crooked smile.

Seeing that smile, Gwi Yang could not smile back. He clenched his fist without realizing it.

At that moment, as if those were not mere bluster, Chung Myung’s sword began to move.

Silence.

The sword planted in the ground was pulled free.

With trembling hands Chung Myung somehow lifted the sword; he wavered briefly but ultimately gripped it with both hands.

Gwi Yang’s eyes shook.

No one would fail to understand what it meant for a swordsman not even to bear the weight of his own sword; at least here, there wasn’t a single person who wouldn’t understand.

Yet he intended to fight. Now Gwi Yang knew as well: this was neither bravado nor a tantrum.

If he could lift the sword, he’d fight. If his life still clung on, he’d resist.

That mode, incomprehensible to some, was perfectly natural to him.

A sharp pain.

Gwi Yang gripped his lower abdomen with his hands as pain surged. The place where Mount Hua’s Sword Saint had stabbed and left a grotesque hole throbbed anew. Other wounds likewise; Gwi Yang had been grievously cut by Chung Myung’s sword.

But Gwi Yang, choosing not to retreat, gripped his sword tighter and stepped forward.

He would cut off Mount Hua’s Sword Saint’s lifeline.

He could not yield this to anyone. It was his right and the recompense for what he had lost.

“...Mount Hua’s Sword Saint.”

Gwi Yang spoke slowly.

“You... you were truly remarkable.”

There was not a hint of sarcasm—only pure admiration.

Even if their paths diverged and aims differed, Gwi Yang was still a swordsman who had taken up the blade. He could not help but show reverence.

“But... you have far too many needless things.”

If that Mount Hua’s Sword Saint had been alone—able to retreat when he wished and fight when he wished—

Could the Blood Sword Troops have captured Mount Hua’s Sword Saint then?

No. If Mount Hua’s Sword Saint had been alone, the Blood Sword Squad would have been thoroughly hunted by him.

They would have cried out in desperation and died miserably, unable to bridge the crushing gap.

This meant that what drove Mount Hua’s Sword Saint to death were the things he sought to protect—trifles that, even combined, didn’t match his worth pushed him toward death.

“It’s a pity. Perhaps... you too could have become an Alliance lord.”

Chung Myung did not answer.

Perhaps he could not answer; his body was like ashes after burning once.

Only a still-burning spirit supported him.

Gwi Yang stepped forward slightly and signaled with his chin. Two Blood Sword Troops members, faces tense, took positions behind him.

He knew Mount Hua’s Sword Saint had not a finger’s worth of strength left. But he’d seen too much to let that alone breed complacency.

Vrrrrrr.

Gwi Yang poured all his remaining inner strength into his sword.

“At least I’ll kill you without pain.”

Fshhhhh!

Grit.

Foolish and stupid.

After a long hunt from Southern Island to here, they had finally driven them into a dead end. Only after so many sacrifices.

‘Green Forest King.’

The Green Forest King must have thought he could manipulate and toy with the situation.

What Ho Ga-Myung exploited was precisely that arrogance of Im So-Byeong.

Make them believe they were choosing everything of their own accord.

A composure they would not dare to imitate. This must be why Mind-Probing Inquisitor Ho Ga-Myung had become famous across the land.

How can the result be the same if the process is different?

“There are more than ten terrains capable of channeling them off Guangdong’s roads.”

Ho Ga-Myung said he chose this method because he couldn’t measure up to Jang Ilso, but to the aide it was impossible to tell which was more remarkable.

If a commoner wanted to enter a contest bearing the great name ‘the world’, he had to struggle until his nails broke and rage relentlessly.

If he could not, he would be unworthy to stand beside Jang Ilso.

Of course the Green Forest King was great. But even Im So-Byeong could not escape the realm of prodigy.

Ho Ga-Myung had long admitted he was a commoner, but Im So-Byeong still brimmed with confidence that his intellect would rival anyone’s.

That difference produced the present result. The convinced do not doubt their own victory.

Ho Ga-Myung slowly unclenched his tightly held fist.

“Attack. Leave not a single one alive—kill them all.”

Ho Ga-Myung smoothed his already parched lips.

There were clearly no variables. But one possibility remained by which he might fail his objective.

The possibility that the men from the Heavenly Friends Alliance would abandon the Southern Island Sect and climb the cliff among themselves to escape.

If they chose that, it would be difficult to end their lives here.

Normally, given the Heavenly Friends Alliance’s conduct, it wasn’t a variable worth considering, but in this moment he had to consider it.

Because it was Mount Hua’s Sword Saint who had lost consciousness.

Mount Hua’s Sword Saint would certainly not back down, but the others were uncertain. At the very end, people reveal their true natures.

But...

Ho Ga-Myung knew: sometimes a person’s value comes from something not measurable by quantity.

That applied not only to them but to Jang Ilso as well.

Could Jang Ilso still be Jang Ilso without his dominance? Could a Jang Ilso who abandoned ambition and chose to be content with reality wield the same influence?

Absolutely impossible.

Even if his force remained the same... no, even if his objective strength grew, Jang Ilso would become someone who could never look beyond that.

Jang Ilso’s value lay in his very dominance.

The same went for them.

If, at the final moment, they abandoned what they tried to protect and barely clung to their lives as they fled, could one still call them the Heavenly Friends Alliance?

They would die in that moment.

They might keep their lives, but the Heavenly Friends Alliance that had matched the Nine Great Sects and even pressured the Four Sects Alliance would cease to exist.

Even if it wasn’t a path Mount Hua’s Sword Saint chose himself, the blame would fall upon him. In that moment, the Justice faction would lose its center.

What will they choose?

Will they honor the grand talks they boast about each time and die to preserve their name? Or will they save their pitiful lives and bury that name in the dung heap?

The Ten Thousand People Clan members’ shouts and the sound of their footsteps pierced his ears like music. Soon someone’s scream would start to mix into it.

‘Is what remains damage control?’

In any case, the fact that he disobeyed Jang Ilso’s order remained unchanged. And that he suffered heavy losses at the hands of a small enemy force also remained unchanged.

So naturally he would have to take responsibility.

Even if the price was his life, Ho Ga-Myung would never regret it.

If his life could bring Jang Ilso one step closer to his great work, he would gladly smile from the afterlife.

A faint smile bloomed on Ho Ga-Myung’s lips. It seemed the first genuine smile since meeting those men.

Some of the Ten Thousand People Clan who had rushed into the canyon of the Ten-Thousand Great Mountains were coming back out and running toward them.

‘What is this?’

A situation report? No, that shouldn’t be necessary now.

“Co-Commander!”

“What’s wrong?”

“Th-they’ve disappeared.”

“What do you mean by that?”

“Th-they’ve disappeared!”

“Disappeared?”

“Yes!”

“Don’t talk nonsense!”

Ho Ga-Myung shouted, enraged. Everyone jumped and looked at him. Truly, they had never seen him raise his voice like that.

“Get out of the way!”

Ho Ga-Myung shoved aside the bowing men as if kicking them away and ran toward the place himself.

‘This is impossible!’

If only the Heavenly Friends Alliance members had vanished while the Southern Island Sect remained, one could understand. But for the Southern Island Sect’s disciples to have disappeared entirely is nonsense.

How could such a thing be possible unless they truly became immortals and worked transformations?

Impossible. Such a thing cannot be!

But shortly after, what Ho Ga-Myung encountered was only the stupid faces of Ten Thousand People Clan members, flustered, bewildered, and looking around.

They were truly gone.

The towering cliffs and steep mountains remained, but there was not a trace of the Southern Island Sect’s men. They had vanished as if rising to the sky.

“Th-this...”

Ho Ga-Myung’s hand began to tremble as if seized by fever.

How? How could this happen? How?

Ho Ga-Myung, always cold and composed, frantically looked around like a madman.

“U... Uu...”

After a moment, he began tearing at his hair. Hair that had been neatly arranged fell loose and disheveled.

When something beyond reason occurs, there’s usually a particular culprit behind it.

“Mount... Hua’s Swooooord...”

A beast-like howl burst from Ho Ga-Myung’s mouth.

“Mount Hua’s Sword Saaaaaaaaaaaint!”

His scream climbed the cliff and rose into the sky, ringing out miserably.

Drip. Drip.

The sound of water droplets falling echoed clearly.

Im So-Byeong looked up. Water had gathered on the jagged rock jutting from the ceiling and was falling drop by drop.

‘Where on earth is this...’

His gaze returned downward.

It was so dark he couldn’t see an inch ahead. Unless one had trained in martial arts and developed qi-sense, the darkness was thick enough to stop anyone from taking a step. The only faint thing visible was a narrow, long path stretching into the distance.

‘A cave... no, a grotto?’

The grotto they had come through extended long behind them.

He was still dumbfounded as to how they had come here. His mind was in turmoil.

“Cough.”

At that moment, Chung Myung, walking ahead of him, coughed and hunched over. Every time he moved, flakes of dried blood crumbled away.

Im So-Byeong hardened his face and spoke.

“Taoist...”

“I’m fine.”

But Chung Myung raised his head as if it were nothing and continued forward.

A sigh escaped Im So-Byeong. Even though he had been offered a piggyback, he stubbornly insisted on walking on his own. Who could break that stubbornness?

After following silently for a while, Im So-Byeong could no longer hold back and asked.

“Taoist... where is this place?”

“A cave.”

“Pardon?”

“Can’t you see? It’s a cave.”

Im So-Byeong’s face twisted slightly.

“No, I’m not asking that. What kind of cave is this?”

Even as he spoke, he felt the question itself was absurd. A cave is a cave; how could it have a special purpose?

But in fact, the question was very reasonable.

Im So-Byeong scanned the cave walls.

No matter how he looked, it didn’t seem naturally formed. It was rough, but clearly touched by human hands.

‘Someone dug a cave this size?’

Why? For what reason?

Then Chung Myung’s voice sounded softly.

“Do you know where this is?”

“...I have no idea.”

“The edge of the Ten Thousand Great Mountains.”

Im So-Byeong’s eyes narrowed slightly. What did that mean...

“Most people think the war with the Demonic Cult was fought over single peaks like a scramble, but in reality thousands of battles took place in the south of the Central Plains, over many years.”

“...”

“The place with the most fighting wasn’t the center of the Ten Thousand Great Mountains. It was the approaches leading to it.”

“So this is...”

“Yes.”

Chung Myung answered in a cold voice.

“It’s one of the hideouts they dug.”

Im So-Byeong looked around again with a stiff face.

‘Someplace like this...’

The Ten Thousand Great Mountains themselves are a natural stronghold. Who would have imagined they’d also bother to create grottoes like this in such mountains?

But there was another reason Im So-Byeong was truly shocked.

From a strategist’s point of view, nothing was more chilling than this grotto. The mountain was already hard to predict where enemies might appear.

And the existence of such holes meant the enemy could burst up from underground.

‘How on earth did they deal with such bastards?’

The moment the Demonic Cult people he had seen in Hangzhou merged with this terrain in his mind, a chill ran through him. The past wars, which he’d only vaguely imagined as brutal, unfolded vividly before him.

Moreover, despite the long time passed, this cold grotto still smelled faintly of blood.

“When you say ‘one of them,’ does that mean there are more than this place?”

“Countless.”

A hollow laugh escaped Im So-Byeong.

Hyun Jong. He stood by the riverbank, gazing toward the distant Southern land.

A stark white bandage was wrapped tight around his chest. Chung Myung, staring silently at the wound, slowly pushed himself up.

A sharp ache.

His face twisted at the intense chest pain. As if the barely healed wound had shifted and gaped again, red blood began to spread across the white bandage.

No sooner had he uttered a shallow breath than there was a loud bang and someone stormed into the room.

“Damn it! How many times is this now, you crazy man?”

“...”

“How many times do I have to tell you, if you wake up and something’s off, just lie still and rest! How many times! Are you a goldfish or what? Did you get hit so many times that your memory’s gone?”

Chung Myung turned his head to the person who had entered. The man’s handsome face was twisted with irritation. Watching that expression, Chung Myung spoke.

“...How long was I unconscious?”

“Three days, three days! This time I thought you were gone for good. Hell, that life of yours is stubborn as hell.”

“Three days...”

Chung Myung snorted a laugh in disbelief.

Usually he’d wake in a day at most, but three days, he must have had one foot in the afterlife.

Well, thinking about it, it made sense.

He hadn’t been fighting alone; facing the high priest while protecting the others was never easy. It was fortunate he had at least crawled back with his life.

“Alcohol.”

“...Has that man lost his mind? Do you even know what being a ‘patient’ means? Even three-year-olds in my village know; don’t they teach that in Shaanxi?”

“No more talk, bring the alcohol.”

“There’s nothing left to drink yourself to death with.”

“Bring it.”

“Ugh, damn!”

A man wearing the green robes that mark the Sichuan Tang Family stormed out. A moment later he returned with a white bottle and hurled it at Chung Myung, snapping angrily.

“Drink and die if you must!”

Thud.

Chung Myung snatched the bottle, opened it, and drank straight from it. The harsh spirit washed into his mouth and slightly quelled the metallic smell of blood rising from his gut.

Of course he knew, no amount of alcohol would make that smell vanish.

The blood smell wasn’t coming from the wound in the first place.

The drink only numbingly distracted him for a moment from the sickening, clinging smell that had soaked into him.

Removing the bottle from his lips, Chung Myung stared blankly at the ceiling. After a long silence he spoke.

“How many made it back?”

“...About forty.”

“How many died?”

“...”

“I asked how many died.”

“About twenty.”

Chung Myung nodded without a word.

It was absurd. Men who’d screamed and fought could reach safety and then drop dead. If you have the strength to swing a sword, you should have the strength to live.

Hearing that twenty of the forty he’d saved were dead left him hollow enough to laugh.

“What about the rest?”

“Now’s not the time to worry about them. You saved them, so they’ll manage. Whether arms are severed or tendons cut, at least they’re alive, what’s the problem?”

“...”

“Worry about yourself, worry about yourself. Or did some ghost from a past life who couldn’t fight latch on to you? How many times have I told you not to stake your life on those bastards?”

“...So much nagging.”

“At least fight while I’m there, didn’t I say that! How many times do you get stabbed in the back fighting those who can’t even guard one another? Taoist’s life is, it’s not dozens or hundreds...”

“Shut up. You’re loud.”

Tang Bo’s face contorted.

Chung Myung drank again. He winced at the burning down his throat, then gave a bitter smile.

“I’ve never gambled with my life.”

“...”

“It just happened while I was fighting.”

Tang Bo, staring at him with a fixed expression, sighed.

“You’ll actually die that way.”

“Is that such a big deal?”

Chung Myung’s gaze shifted to the window beside the bed. The world outside was impossibly blue, and impossibly desolate.

“Hundreds, thousands die each day, what difference would adding my life make?”

“So you’re trying to die sooner?”

Chung Myung looked at Tang Bo blankly. Tang Bo didn’t shrink back; instead he raised his voice angrily.

“Why risk your life for people you don’t even get along with! You always complain those damned bastards give you stomach-aches, what, are you two-faced?”

Chung Myung gave a small laugh at that; there was no real argument to give.

“Because blood rushes to my head.”

“...”

“That won’t happen anymore.”

“Yeah, right, sure.”

Tang Bo let out a long, sinking sigh.

Even after knowing him so long, he sometimes couldn’t understand this Taoist.

He wasn’t particularly virtuous, nor was he someone chasing lofty Taoist ideals.

Objectively, he was the kind of Taoist they wondered how on earth ever joined the sect.

So why did he always make those choices at crucial moments?

“Taoist.”

“What?”

“I’m not joking, you’ll actually die that way.”

“...”

“You know it, if even you go down, we can’t win this war. No matter how many others die, you have to stay alive somehow.”

“I know.”

“Hyung.”

“I said I know.”

Tang Bo, hesitating, looked steadily into Chung Myung’s eyes and said.

“Not all those in the Demonic Cult are the same.”

“What do you mean?”

“Not every Demonic Cult member you behead is the one who killed Mount Hua’s Taoists. And besides, killing them won’t bring the dead back...”

“Shut your mouth.”

In that moment Chung Myung’s gaze changed. In the chill of his eyes, Tang Bo could no longer press him.

Tang Bo sighed again, full of complaint.

“You should keep your mouth shut.”

He pulled a medicinal paste from his robe and tossed it at Chung Myung, who caught it reflexively.

“Apply it to the wound. I roughly stitched you up, so if you treat it with the paste it shouldn’t let it reopen. Don’t do anything foolish today, lie still.”

Tang Bo turned and started to leave, but abruptly stopped and, without looking back, said:

“Those, the men you rescued.”

“...What about them?”

“They wanted me to tell you they’re grateful.”

“...”

Thunk.

Tang Bo closed the door and left.

Lying back down, Chung Myung stared at the ceiling. As feeling slowly returned to his body, the nicks and bruises ached. But he was used to that pain now.

‘Has he lost his mind?’

Chung Myung laughed aloud.

Wasn’t that the most obvious thing to say?

In this crazy world, there’s no one left completely sane. Everyone is clutching at a half-lost mind.

He knew. He knew, and that’s why he acted this way. The dead do not come back.

No matter the regret or struggle, those who die once never return.

He knew all too well how cruel that was.

He felt as if he were sinking ever deeper. In that strange but familiar sensation, Chung Myung opened his eyes.

A dark ceiling, the smell of blood, the pain sweeping through his body, an intense déjà vu washed over him.

Pushing up his leaden eyelids, Chung Myung raised his head to look at his body.

A bandage was wrapped around his chest.

It was familiar yet unnerving, the body beneath the bandage looked smaller and frailer than the body he once knew.

Chung Myung’s gaze slowly turned to the side.

Someone slept propped against the unfamiliar cave wall.

The sleeper’s face shifted from one he recognized into a stranger’s.

Staring dazedly at Baek Cheon’s face, neatly resting even in sleep, Chung Myung lifted his gaze again.

‘...Right.’

He was no longer the Plum Blossom Sword Saint.

The Mount Hua he knew was gone.

He had won the war, but lost everything in return.

All he had tried to protect was gone, and all he hoped to leave behind had vanished.

What remained here was only...

Ssshk. Ssshk.

As he drifted back, a small breath reached his ear. Turning his head, he saw Yu Yiseol lying nearby and Tang Soso sleeping on the other side.

They looked as if they’d fallen asleep after exhausting themselves checking on him.

For a long time Chung Myung quietly stared at Tang Soso’s face, as if trying to find traces of the past there.

But it didn’t work.

Tang Bo’s face from his earlier dream felt distant. For some time now, the man’s features often failed to come clearly to his mind.

Sometimes brutally vivid, then fading in an instant, growing dull, bit by bit.

“You’re awake?”

No answer was really necessary; it wasn’t a question that required one.

What needed saying was no different than before.

“...How long has it been?”

“Not exactly sure, but probably about two days.”

“Two days...”

Better than three?

Chung Myung smiled and tried to sit up, but Baek Cheon pressed down hard on his forehead.

“Don’t let the barely-healed wound burst open, lie still and behave.”

“It’s healed.”

Clench.

Baek Cheon silently drew his sword. Chung Myung finally relaxed his neck. Baek Cheon might not be stronger than Tang Bo, but he had already proven himself to be even less sensible.

Keeping his head on the ground, Chung Myung rolled his eyes and spoke.

“What about the others?”

“Gul and Yiseol were badly hurt, but still they are better than you. Don’t worry about others, look after yourself.”

“Southern Island?”

“...You really don’t understand, do you?”

“I asked how the Southern Island is.”

At the persistent question, Baek Cheon sighed deeply.

“Half.”

“...The elders?”

“The same.”

That meant about half dead, counting those who never made it here and those who arrived only to die.

Judging by Baek Cheon’s expression, the real death toll was likely far above half, but either way it was grim.

If that was the cost of breaking through Guangdong, the Ten Thousand People Clan’s stronghold, then those losses weren’t a complete disaster. If anything, it could be called a success.

Of course, no one would put it that way.

Chung Myung pushed himself up again.

“I told you to lie down.”

“I’m really fine now.”

Chung Myung pushed Baek Cheon’s hand off his head and sat up. Baek Cheon tried to stop him but finally shook his head.

“I was the one who put the rear in your hands.”

At that unexpected remark, Chung Myung stared at Baek Cheon.

“So I know I don’t have the right to tell you this.”

“...What are you talking about all of a sudden?”

“But I have to ask, why push yourself that far?”

“...”

“You could have pulled back. Even if more people died because of that choice, no one would have blamed you. You know that, so why force it? Why?”

Baek Cheon’s lips trembled. He appeared calm, but clearly wasn’t.

Watching him, Chung Myung suddenly turned his head toward the still-unconscious Yu Yiseol.

“It wasn’t forcing things.”

“...What?”

“I just believed.”

“When I fall past my limits, I believed there would always be someone behind me, that’s all.”

Only now, finally, had there been people he could lean his back against.
